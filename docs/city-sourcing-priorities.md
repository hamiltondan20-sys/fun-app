## Demand Estimates, Not Keyword-Tool Results

The numbers below are **low-confidence editorial estimates**, not measurements
from Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs, Semrush, Search Console, or a paid API.
No search-volume dataset was available for this pass. The source of the estimates
is editorial judgment about US travel-search demand. The tourism-board links
support the sourcing assessment only; they do not substantiate the volume ranges.

Assumed market: United States, English queries, Google Search, average month
rather than a seasonal peak. These are rough planning bands, not statistical
confidence intervals or a forecast of traffic this site will receive. Actual
volume may fall outside them. Adjacent ranks are especially uncertain.

This was prepared as a priority list **within the 147 already-published destinations in the
5-9 classifier band**, limited to city or town guides. It is not a claim that
these are the world's 20 most-searched cities. High-demand guides outside that
band, such as London and Paris, still need source review. Held-back cities such
as Nashville and Atlanta should enter a separate comparison when measured
keyword data is available; their research leads are not completed verification.

Order follows estimated combined demand for the three queries, using the sum
of range midpoints as a rough sorting aid. Ties are editorial choices, not a
measured distinction. Sourcing convenience does not move a city above a higher
demand tier. Adding the three estimates is not a count of unique searchers.

Before allocating the entire 80-140 hours implied by 20 cities, validate the
60 queries against one consistent keyword export. Use US, English, Google only,
and the same trailing 12 months for every query. Keep the export date, settings,
close-variant treatment, and source alongside the results. Do not mix global
volumes with US volumes. An existing Keyword Planner account can provide
historical estimates; do not buy data or activate a campaign for this plan.
Google's current help says account setup including billing details is required
for basic Keyword Planner access, so this is not a zero-setup dependency.

References: [Google Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243?hl=en-GB),
[location and language settings](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6325025?hl=en),
[historical metrics and close variants](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/keyword-planning/generate-historical-metrics).
These explain measurement, not the estimates below.

## Completion Means a Reviewed City

1. Start with the linked official tourism source, then find each venue's own site.
2. Confirm the correct city, country, branch, category, and current operating evidence.
3. Record exact name, primary URL, source type, verification date and supporting evidence.
   Add a genuinely independent second source where available, not two pages from one owner.
4. Preserve specific activity descriptions. Do not rewrite a valid neighborhood
   walk just because it is not a business name. Record the underlying location
   and why the wording is supported.
5. Leave an unsupported field empty instead of inventing a replacement. If an
   existing validation rule rejects the incomplete data, report the conflict;
   do not fill it generically or weaken a gate to finish the city.
6. Check every current recommendation, not just enough entries to raise a count.
   Relevant repetition across categories is acceptable, but is not another venue.
7. Update the source ledger, regenerate the verification inventory, run the full
   release chain and tests, then commit the reviewed content from this checkout.
