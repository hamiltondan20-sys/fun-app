## Planning Rate, Not Measured Throughput

Use the previously discussed 4-7 focused sourcing hours per city only as an
estimate; it has not been timed. Writing, tests, approval and deployment add
effort. A planning capacity of one or two completed reviews per week requires
roughly 4-14 sourcing hours weekly, plus those additional tasks.

| Capacity assumption | All 189 from scratch | Remaining 179 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One city weekly | 189 weeks, about 3.6 years | 179 weeks, about 3.4 years |
| Two cities weekly | 95 weeks, about 1.8 years | 90 weeks, about 1.7 years |
| Three cities weekly | 63 weeks, about 1.2 years | 60 weeks, about 1.1 years |

These are straight-line capacity scenarios, not promises. Maintenance, failed
retrievals, approval delays and revisiting stale evidence lengthen the work.
Start recording actual sourcing and QA hours for the next three reviews before
treating any rate as an observed benchmark.

## Options

1. Continue individual reviews. Exact branches and readable operator/location
   checks remain mandatory. The backlog takes years at one city weekly, with
   older reviews needing maintenance before completion.
2. Batch related cities. Share tourism-board discovery, language and validation
   setup. Keep per-place checks and evidence unchanged; batching alone need not
   cost verification quality. Cutting operator/location checks, using snippets
   instead of opened sources, or skipping operating evidence would cost quality
   and is not recommended. No measured batch-speed multiplier is available.
3. Make source status more visible. Explain classifier-passed versus reviewed
   beside each guide, with date and scope only where evidence exists. This is a
   proposed rendering change requiring separate approval. Do not describe
   unreviewed content as wrong or pretend its names were source-verified.

## Current About Page and Recommendation

The current About page already says: "Source reviews are still in progress.
Some published guides have passed automated content checks but have not
completed a recorded source review. An automated match for a place name is not
proof that the place exists or is currently open." It also states: "we do not
claim that every published guide has been fully source-checked."

Thus the premise that About unequivocally claims all 189 were reviewed is not
what the current source says. Its opening process description may still lead
readers to generalise if they do not read the later qualification.

Recommendation: retain the evidence standard, use modest related-city batches,
measure actual effort, and separately seek approval for prominent per-guide
status wording. Honest coverage disclosure should not wait for a multi-year
backlog to finish. No About text or status display was changed in this release.
